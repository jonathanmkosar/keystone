## What was done

The full profile: for one person, 5,000 chats over three years, 1 to 39 turns each of about 220 words, seven in ten on a hosted model, and one chat of 600 turns on a local model whose window it outgrows; for five more people, 200 chats each. Every chat has a recap and the call that wrote it; one in 25 an upload; one in 40 an artifact in two versions.

The request in the audit log and the record in `turn_contexts` come from the real context builder; events are the turn package's own types. **The proof**: `TestTheFastPathLeavesWhatARealTurnLeaves` sends six chats through the real handler for one person, writes the same six with the fast path for another, and compares the two databases row by row, every column but how long a call took and its month, with random ids replaced by their order. It passed the first time it ran, which says the turn's rows are regular; the test then changes one word of one event and expects one difference, so it is known to see one.
- **The measurements**, thirteen, each against the real handler (`app.Open`, in memory, the fake provider with no delay), thirty runs each, as a median and a 95th percentile: context packing in the long chat and in a chat with a project, an upload and attached chats; `search_chats` inside a real turn and the chat search route; the chat list and the long chat's page; the first streamed words and the time to stream a reply, for one person and for all six sending at once; the usage report, the Operations report and the home screen's notice.

- **The Monitor comparison**, twice a run: the database's bytes, the files, the artifacts, the cap and its spend, each model's calls, cost and median latency, and the twenty newest traces, each against a count the test kept itself from what it wrote and what its own turns streamed.

## Findings

1. **Past-chat search over a common word took seconds. Over budget, fixed.** Over 100,000 messages a search for two common words took 2.5 seconds through the route and 1.2 to 2 seconds through `search_chats`. Both queries joined every match to its message and chat and cut a snippet from each before keeping the best fifty, and a common word matched 87,691 of 102,836 messages. Now the index gives its best few in its own order and only those are cut and read; at most `chats.search_rank_max` (20,000) matches are ranked, the newest matches being given when more match; and a search for any of several words leaves a word that common out while another is in fewer messages, so a rare word still finds its old chat (decision 0530, `internal/chat/ranked.go`, `TestASearchRanksAtMostSoManyMatches`). Over 205,000 messages the route's 95th percentile is 117 ms and the tool's 269 ms.
2. **A request's usage records were found by reading every record. Fixed before the first full run, so what it cost at full size was never measured.** `usage.List` sent one query for every filter, `(? = '' OR month = ?) AND (? = '' OR request_id = ?)`, which SQLite can only answer by scanning the table: 125 ms for one request among 55,000 records, and the Operations report asks once for each of twenty traces. `List` now puts in only the conditions that are set, and `TestListFindsAMonthAndARequestByTheirIndex` reads the query's plan. After it, one request's records take half a millisecond.
3. **The home screen's notice read a whole usage report to show two sums. Inside its budget by a tenth, made lighter.** In the first full run `GET /api/v1/operations/watch` took 100 ms at the median and 136 ms at the 95th percentile, of a budget of 150: `Limits` called `usagereport.Read`, which places every record of the month by its chat, for the cap's spend and the reserve's. It now calls `usagereport.Standing`, the same cap and reserve read the same way without the breakdowns (`TestStandingIsTheReportsCapAndReserve` holds them equal). The second full run: 31 and 42 ms, and the Operations report went from 111 and 129 ms to 38 and 50.
4. **A database is about 70 KB a turn, and five sixths of it is not the chat. The room is raised; two owner questions.** `audit_log` is 58% (4.12 GB): every model call's entry holds the whole request, the tools' definitions and the chat so far, again for each turn. `turn_events` is 25% (1.79 GB, 15 million rows): every streamed piece of every reply, about 150 rows a turn, kept for good. `turn_contexts` is 6%. The messages and their search index are 8%. The shipped room of 2 GB is eleven months of the load's person; `operations.database_room_mb` is now 10240 (decision 0533).
5. **Every API call opens the person's store anew. Inside every budget, not fixed.** `signin.Resolve` calls `Install.Store` for each request: a new SQLite connection, the migration check and two folder handles, under the install's one lock. It is a fixed part of every call's time, the chat list's 9 ms among them, and the likely reason the first words take 80 ms with six people sending at once and 25 ms alone; the lock was not timed apart. A store kept open for each person would take it away. It belongs to the API shell and the turn runner together, so it is left as a note.
6. **Context packing in a 600-turn chat takes 110 ms, and grows with the chat. Inside its budget, not fixed.** A turn reads the chat's whole current branch more than once before its model call (to find where the message goes, then to build the request), and decodes every message each time. At 600 turns that is under half the budget; a chat of two thousand turns would be near it.

Nothing was found wrong in what Operations reports: all 21 figures matched in both full runs and in every small run.

## What was learned

- The plain turn is regular enough to write by hand and prove: the fast path matched the real handler row for row at the first comparison. What made that possible is that the request and its record come from `contextbuild.Build` and the events from the turn's own types; only the order of the rows and the columns are the fast path's own.
- The real paths take no date, and the audit log should not: so a load with a past needs a writer of its own, and that writer needs its proof.
- SQLite's FTS5 keeps `ORDER BY rank LIMIT n` to itself only when the query is on the index alone, with that one order. A join, a second sort key or a subquery by row id each makes it rank, or cut, everything. And a prefix search (`"word"*`) is set up again for every separate lookup, so "fetch these fifty by id from the index" cost fifty set-ups; the snippet has to come from the one ranked query.
- `(? = '' OR column = ?)` reads as one query for every filter and costs a scan of the table. There may be others like it; `usage.List` was the one a measured path ran into.
- The first large load was the first time anything in Keystone met a table of a hundred thousand rows. Two of the four changes were found at 12,000 to 50,000 turns, with loads made in under three minutes (`go run ./tools/loadtest -chats 600`), before the full run was ever made.
- A hosted model cannot be the fake adapter (an in-process adapter is local by rule). The load's hosted model is a Chat Completions entry at `models.invalid`, which can never resolve, and the rig hands that adapter the fake provider, as the isolation drill does with its own model.
- Writing slows as the database grows: 25 seconds for the first 10,000 turns, and 50 to 80 for each 10,000 past the sixth. The cause was not looked into; it is the generator's time, not a person's.
- Two full runs differ by more than their medians suggest: streaming for one person was 26 ms and then 69 ms at the median, `search_chats` 197 ms and then 269 ms at the 95th percentile. Both were far inside their budgets both times; the budgets that held closest are context packing in the long chat (165 of 250) and the two searches.
