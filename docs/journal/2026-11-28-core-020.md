## What was learned

- The verdicts already carry the turn's request id, which is how the report places every usage record, so "which calls were on Auto" needed no new link between usage and turns: one join and one index.
- A turn's verdicts and its calls can fall in different months. The first fault put in by hand, counting a turn by any verdict in the period, passed the generated test until one turn was made to be routed twice across midnight; the world now always has one.
- The test tenant's recorded steps are in no registry (decision 0695), so its top step cannot be priced as shipped. The proof adds a registry entry under the large step's name, which the test tenant's turns never read.
- With five agents' `verify` runs on the machine at once, the load test's streaming budgets (1 s) and the real-browser test (Edge) failed on runs that changed nothing they measure; see "What was verified".

## Questions, each with a recommended answer

1. **Auto's cost counts the router's decision calls, and the top step's does not.** Recommended: keep it; it is what Auto cost against what not having Auto would have cost, and a local decision model costs nothing. Built: as recommended (decision 0741).
2. **The cache counts are priced as they happened** on the step that answered; always using the top step would have read its cache more. Recommended: keep the plain comparison and say so in the decision; modelling caches nobody wrote would be a guess. Built: as recommended (decision 0742).
3. **A past period is priced at today's ladder and price.** Recommended: keep it, as the cap is. Built: as recommended.
