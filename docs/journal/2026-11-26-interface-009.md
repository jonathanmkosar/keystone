## What was done

- **The chart format**, decision 0540: a fenced `chart` block holding one JSON object, with a `type` among bar, line, area, pie and scatter, a `title`, `x` and `y` with their titles and `x.labels`, and named `series` of `values` (or `points` for scatter). `web/app/src/chart.ts`, with no imports: `parseChart` checks it against the settings' limits and refuses anything else with one sentence; `layoutChart` lays it out at a given size as plain shapes, ticks, a legend and the places a pointer can rest; `tipFor`, `chartSummary`, `chartTable` and `niceScale`.
- **Charts on the screen**: `InlineVisuals.tsx` measures its width and builds SVG elements from the layout. Bars that stand on zero, lines with a pattern each, areas with a wash, a pie with its shares in the legend, a scatter with a shape for each series; a note under the pointer; the image's name is the title and the summary; a Data panel with the summary and a real table; a Source panel.
- **Diagrams**, decision 0541: a fenced `mermaid` block is drawn by Mermaid in an `iframe` with `sandbox="allow-scripts"`. A new package, `internal/visuals`, serves the page, `GET /api/v1/visuals/diagram?theme=`: the same for every diagram, holding the artifact sandbox's Mermaid renderer and no diagram, under the sandbox's policy with the script named by its SHA-256 digest in place of `'unsafe-inline'`. `sandbox/mermaid.ts` gained a mode that waits for a diagram from the page that frames it. The page and the frame talk through `visuals.ts`: `ready`, `draw` (the source, the theme, thirteen token colours, each checked to be a hex colour), `drawn`, `problem`. Mermaid's `base` theme is given the tokens.
- **Saving a diagram**: `POST /api/v1/chats/{id}/visuals/diagrams` makes a Mermaid artifact of the chat through `artifacts.Create`, and the button opens it beside the chat.
- **In the reply**: `Markdown.tsx` records whether a fenced block was closed and offers it to a drawer (`FenceDrawer`); only `Conversation.tsx` gives one, by wrapping a reply's text in `InlineVisuals`. `planBlock` decides: a code block, a placeholder of the height the visual will draw at while the block is still being written, the chart, the diagram's frame, or the source with a plain note. A frame is made when its diagram is within 600 pixels of the window, and one that does not answer within `visuals.diagram_wait` becomes its source with a note. Larger lifts the box over the page and holds its place.
- **The model is told**, decision 0542: `internal/turn/visuals.go` adds `visuals.instructions` to the standing text while `visuals.draw` is on, by one line in `context.go` before the search-first rule.
- **Settings**: a new `settings/visuals.json`, lane `visuals`, nine settings.
- **The test tenant**: `turns/inline-visuals.json` with three recorded turns (a bar chart; a flowchart; one of each other kind, a sequence diagram and a block that is not a chart) and the chat `chats/04-kayak-hires.json`.
- **The proof**: `web/app/test/chart.test.ts` and `visuals.test.ts`, 27 tests, run by `npm test`; `go test ./internal/visuals` and two tests in `internal/turn`; a new standing check, `inline visuals`, in `tools/verify/inlinevisuals.go`. `verify` is 39 of 39.
- The write-up: `docs/architecture/inline-visuals.md` with its `componentDocs` lines and overview row, decisions 0540 to 0544, a section in `web.md`, and a paragraph each in `tools.md`, `test-tenant.md` and `artifacts.md`.

## What was learned

- **Windows does not tell `Visuals.tsx` from `visuals.ts`**, as interface 008 found with dictation: `import "./Visuals"` would have resolved to the rules file. The screen's part is `InlineVisuals.tsx`.
- **The desktop app's browser pane refuses a sandboxed frame's request, and a headless Edge does not.** Edge was started with `--headless=new --remote-debugging-port` and a throwaway profile, and driven from a Node script over the debugging port with Node's own `WebSocket` and `fetch`: sign in through the API, open the chat, read the page's state, take pictures. No package was needed. That is how the frame was seen drawing inside a reply.
- **A page's one script can be megabytes and still be named by its digest.** Mermaid at its `strict` level needs no inline script of its own, so `script-src 'sha256-…'` with no `'unsafe-inline'` drew every diagram tried.
- **A frame is transparent only when its colour scheme matches the page's**, so the diagram's page takes the theme in its address and sets `color-scheme` to it; the reply's ground then shows through.
- **A chart cannot be scaled to fit a phone**: at 345 pixels a 640-wide drawing has 6-pixel text. It is laid out at the width it has, which is why the layout is a function of width and height.
- **The tokens have no chart palette.** The six that serve were put in the order that keeps neighbours apart under simulated colour-blindness; they still fail a palette check on two counts (most read as nearly grey, and in the light theme `strong` and `ok` are close), so each series has a second mark. Decision 0544.
- **One assertion in the chats lane's tests compared the first system block with the standing text exactly.** It now compares with the standing text and the visuals' words.
- Adding a chat to the test tenant broke nothing: the loader's and the checks' counts come from the fixtures. Its words avoid "ferry" and "windproof", which two search cases expect in one chat each.
- Tests that passed at once were broken on purpose in a copy: with `planBlock` drawing a streaming block as code, and with `readFrameMessage` not asking for the channel, two tests failed.
