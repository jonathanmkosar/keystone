# Chats 003, 2026-11-25

Item done: P5-01, the context builder.

## What was done

- A new package, `internal/contextbuild`, with one function: `Build(ctx, Input) (seam.Request, Record, error)`. It imports the seam, the budget, the registry's `Model` and the settings registry, and nothing that touches a network or a store.
- The parts of a call, in the plan's order of priority: rules and tools, the current turn, attachments and attached chats, earlier turns, recalled knowledge, the summary of older history. The standing start of a call also holds the profile, the person map, the project and the style.
- Only the rules, the current turn and the earlier turns have content today. Every other part is a named field of `Input`, empty for now, and is already packed and recorded: `Tools`, `Profile`, `PersonMap`, `Project`, `Style`, `Attached`, `Recall`, `Summary`.
- Packing: what is never left out goes in first. Then attachments up to a share, then earlier turns from the newest back, each whole, stopping at the first that does not fit, then recall and the summary inside shares that are held for them. When turns are left out the model is told in one sentence.
- A turn is a message of the person's and everything up to their next. `Turns` cuts a branch only where no tool call is waiting for its result.
- The count is the budget's estimate, piece by piece. The packed request is then counted once by `budget.Tokens`, and packed again smaller if a provider counts it over.
- The record lists every part with its state, tokens, and pieces kept and dropped, and every turn of the branch with its tokens and whether it was sent.
- In `internal/turn`: `Runner.work` calls a new method, `Runner.build`, in a new file, `context.go`, where it built the request. The record of each model call is stored in a new table, `turn_contexts`, and `turn.Contexts` reads them back. One migration, `20261125T070000_turn_contexts.sql`.
- Eight settings in `settings/chats.json`, all tenant scope: the standing instructions, the fill, the most earlier turns, the three shares, the note, and the recounts.
- In `verify`: `context fit` gained the packing step the plan words it by, in `tools/verify/contextpack.go`.
- The doc `docs/architecture/context.md`, a row in the overview, a line in `componentDocs`, a row in `internal/README.md`, and decisions 0170 to 0173.

## What was learned

- Read strictly, the plan's order lets a long chat take all the room with its own turns, and the summary is dropped in the one case it exists for. Recall and the summary are each held a share ahead of the turns (decision 0171).
- The estimates of the pieces each round up, so their sum is never less than the estimate of the whole. That is what lets the builder pack piece by piece and still be certain the guard will pass the request.
- The margin is added to the whole, so the builder packs under the largest count whose count with the margin is within the limit, and not under the limit itself.
- The note needs room too. When the first packing leaves a turn out, the turns are packed once more with the note's tokens taken off.
- A provider's count can only be asked of a whole request. Asking it for every candidate would be a network call for each turn weighed. One count after packing, and a second packing in the rare case it is over, costs one call in the usual case (decision 0172).
- The record of a long chat is large, because it lists every turn. That is why it is a row beside the turn and not an event in the stream the chat screen reads (decision 0173).
- A test in `internal/turn` told a title call from a chat call by whether the request had system text. Every chat call has system text now. The test still passed, with the title's words as the chat's reply, which is the kind of pass that hides a change. It now tells the two apart by the title call's own message.
- A stop that arrives while a provider is counting comes back from the builder as an error. The runner reads it as a stop, not a failure.

## What was verified

On Windows 11 with Go 1.26, in the worktree for this item:

- `gofmt -l` prints nothing for `internal`, `tools` and `cmd`.
- `go test ./internal/contextbuild` and `go test ./internal/turn` pass.
- `go run ./tools/verify` is 15 of 15, before the build commit and before the write-up commit.

The item's proof, each a test:

- Packing, by priority. A chat that fits is sent whole. The oldest turns are left out whole, with no gap, and the next older turn would not have fitted. A tool call is never parted from its result, at every window from one that holds almost nothing to one that holds everything. The current turn and the standing start are never left out, and a message larger than the window is returned over and refused by the budget by the same number. Attachments, recall and the summary keep their shares against a long chat, lose them at a share of zero, and a pinned item stays where an unpinned one goes.
- The record matches what the model received. Every test puts the request together again from the record and the input alone and compares the two. The record's total is the budget's count of the request, and each part and turn is the estimate of its own piece. In `internal/turn`, the stored record of a real turn agrees with the audit log's copy of what was sent.
- In `verify`, for each of the six fixture models: the largest test chat is packed whole under the window, and a generated chat four times the window is packed under it with only its oldest turns left out, by the estimate and by a provider that counts half as much again.

Not run: `go run ./tools/rebuild`, the binary in a browser, any system but Windows, and any real model. The race detector was not run.
